6 questions to ask any IT recycling company before you book a collection

Not all IT recycling companies operate to the same standard. These six questions will help you identify whether a provider can actually protect your business before you hand over your equipment.

How to choose an IT recycling company – why the right questions matter

The IT recycling market contains a wide range of providers. At one end are fully accredited specialists with documented procedures, vetted staff and a complete audit trail. At the other are informal operators with little more than a van and a willingness to take equipment off your hands. The problem is that both look similar at the point of booking, and the difference only becomes apparent later – when a data breach surfaces, when an auditor asks for documentation, or when equipment turns out to have been processed through unregulated channels.

Knowing how to choose an IT recycling company before you book is the most effective way to protect your business. Answers to the six questions below will help you identify whether a provider can genuinely meet your compliance and data security requirements, or whether they are likely to create a problem further down the line. Our article on what truly sustainable IT recycling looks like covers the broader criteria for evaluating providers if you want to go deeper.

Why due diligence matters before you book

IT recycling is not a commodity purchase where all providers are interchangeable. The consequences of choosing a provider that falls short are not visible at the point of booking. They only really appear afterwards: a data breach if devices are not properly sanitised, WEEE non-compliance if equipment is not processed through authorised channels, and no documentation to defend the business if disposal is later challenged by the Environment Agency or the ICO.

IT recycling company due diligence before booking is the only reliable way to avoid these outcomes. Our article on legal obligations when disposing of old IT assets covers the specific compliance risks in more detail.

Question 1: are you licensed and authorised as a WEEE treatment facility?

What you are looking for is a direct answer naming the relevant authorisation from the Environment Agency or Scottish Environment Protection Agency – not a vague reference to being registered or compliant. WEEE registration as a producer or broker is not the same as being an authorised treatment facility. Only authorised treatment facilities can legally process WEEE for final disposal. A provider that cannot name their specific authorisation and the issuing agency warrants further scrutiny.

Question 2: where is the equipment actually processed?

For companies concerned about sustainable and ethical waste management look for a clear statement that processing takes place only in the UK with no equipment exported to third countries. Equipment exported for processing in countries with weaker environmental regulation can end up in informal operations that cause environmental harm and provide no audit trail. UK-based processing also means your data-bearing devices are handled under UK data protection standards throughout.

Saving Tech Waste: All equipment we collect is processed in the UK. No devices are exported for handling at any stage.

Question 3: what data destruction method do you use and can you evidence it?

Look for companies promoting a named sanitisation standard – NCSC-approved methods are the benchmark for UK businesses – and confirmation that options for Certificates of Data Destruction can be issued per device, not just per collection. A blanket certificate covering a whole collection does not provide the audit trail some UK GDPR compliance requires. Factory resets, formatting and standard deletion are not secure data destruction. Data can often be recovered from devices that have not been properly sanitised using widely available tools.

You can view how our secure data destruction works and examples of our certificates on our website before making any commitment.

Question 4: what documentation will I receive and what does it cover?

A Certificate of Recycling confirming WEEE-compliant processing, together with a Certificate of Data Destruction for every data-bearing device or collection, should be the minimum documentation you expect from your recycling provider. Ask to see examples before booking a collection. If a provider cannot demonstrate the certificates they issue upfront, there is no guarantee they will provide them reliably once your equipment has been collected.

Without this documentation, you have no formal evidence to demonstrate that your equipment was handled and disposed of correctly, leaving you exposed if an auditor, regulator or customer later asks you to prove what happened to your IT assets.

Saving Tech Waste: Certificates of Recycling and Data Destruction are issued for every collection. Examples are available on our website before any commitment is made.

Question 5: what is your policy on landfill?

Look for a clear commitment that no equipment goes to landfill, with an explanation of how all material streams are handled downstream. WEEE legislation prohibits landfill disposal of electrical and electronic equipment, but not all providers avoid it in practice. Some direct difficult-to-process items to landfill when proper processing costs are too high. A credible provider should be able to explain what happens to every category of equipment they collect, including items that are not cost-effective to recycle at market rates.

Question 6: are your staff background-checked and are vehicles tracked?

Get confirmation that staff handling data-bearing devices have been through background checks, and that collection vehicles are tracked throughout the journey from your premises to the processing facility. The chain of custody must be maintained from the moment devices leave your building. A provider with unvetted staff or untracked vehicles cannot guarantee that chain of custody, and cannot evidence it if challenged.

A note on the cost of IT recycling

Cost is not on this list because it is not a reliable indicator of quality in either direction. Understanding what to look for in an IT recycling company goes well beyond cost. A free collection service can be fully compliant, well-documented and run to a high standard. A paid service may still fall short on data destruction standards, documentation or landfill policy. Evaluate providers on the six questions above before considering cost.

Frequently asked questions

What if a provider cannot answer one of these questions?

That is itself useful information. Knowing how to vet IT asset disposal companies means recognising that a credible provider should answer all six questions directly and without hesitation. Vague responses, deflection or an unwillingness to share examples of certificates are red flags worth taking seriously.

Should I ask these questions even if the provider has good online reviews?

Yes. Reviews reflect other customers' experiences, but don't confirm that a specific IT recycler meets your specific compliance, security and environmental requirements. Always ask the questions directly before booking.

Can I ask for a trial collection before committing to an ongoing arrangement?

That is a reasonable request and a credible provider should accommodate it. A single collection will allow you to assess the documentation quality and process before committing to a longer-term arrangement. Part of knowing how to choose an IT recycling company is being willing to test the provider against these criteria before committing.

Is there a quick way to check a provider's WEEE authorisation?

Yes. Environment Agency registrations are publicly searchable. Ask the provider for their registration number and verify it directly. A provider who is reluctant to share this information is not one you should be handing data-bearing devices to.
