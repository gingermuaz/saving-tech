How to catalogue and prepare your IT assets before recycling

Good IT asset inventory management starts before the recycler arrives. This guide covers what to capture, how to build an inventory, and how to prepare for collection day.

The IT asset record problem most organisations face before recycling

Most organisations approaching an IT refresh or office clear-out discover the same problem: the asset records are incomplete, out of date, or do not exist at all. Equipment has been moved between departments, stored in cupboards, or replaced without being logged. That is a common situation, and it is not a barrier to recycling. Even partial cataloguing before your collection improves the process significantly – for your team, for your recycler, and for your compliance record.

This guide sets out why IT asset inventory management matters before a recycling collection, what information to catalogue equipment before recycling, and how to prepare equipment so that collection day runs smoothly.

Why cataloguing your assets before recycling matters

Good IT asset inventory management before a recycling collection delivers benefits across several areas at once.

From a compliance perspective, a documented asset record with serial numbers and disposal dates satisfies auditor and regulatory requirements. If you are ever asked to prove what was disposed of and when, a pre-collection inventory is the foundation of that evidence. Combined with the Certificates of Data Destruction and Recycling issued after collection, it gives you a complete audit trail from asset to disposal.

From a data security perspective, cataloguing allows you to identify which devices held sensitive data – shared departmental machines, devices used by specific roles, or equipment connected to particular systems, and ensure they are flagged for appropriate destruction methods.

From an operational perspective, a catalogued list makes collection faster and more accurate. Collectors can verify equipment against your inventory rather than discovering what you have on the day, which reduces errors and speeds up the process.

Cataloguing also supports refurbishment decisions. Knowing the age, condition, and specification of each device makes it easier to assess which items might be suitable for reuse rather than material recycling. Our article on refurbishment vs recycling covers how that assessment works in practice.

What information to capture for each device

The goal of IT asset management best practices at this stage is to capture enough information to identify each device uniquely, confirm its condition, and link it to a destruction record after collection. You do not need a perfect record for every field, even a partial inventory is far more useful than none.

For each device, record the device type (laptop, desktop, server, monitor, printer, tablet, mobile phone, switch, or other), the make and model, and the serial number. Serial numbers are the most important field. They are what allows a destruction certificate to be matched to a specific device in your records, and what auditors use to verify individual assets.

Where possible, also record the internal asset tag or ID number, the department or business unit that used the device, the condition (working, non-functional, damaged, or specific fault notes), and the date of planned disposal. For larger collections or regulated environments, it is worth adding a data classification field – noting whether the device handled sensitive data such as customer records, financial information, or employee personal data – and a backup status confirmation.

Supporting fields such as operating system version, last service date, and users who had access are helpful for regulated sectors or where devices are being assessed for refurbishment suitability, but they are not essential for a standard collection. These are the core IT asset management best practices that apply regardless of organisation size.

Tools for capturing asset information

The right tool depends on the size of your collection and what you already have in place.

A spreadsheet

Excel or Google Sheets – is the simplest and most accessible option, and works well for collections of up to around 100 devices. Create one row per device and one column per data field. It can be shared easily with your IT team and provided to your recycler ahead of collection. We have a recycling checklist available on our website that can serve as a starting point for your inventory structure.

Asset management software

If your organisation already uses an IT asset management system such as ServiceNow, Jira, or a similar platform, use the asset management module to export a report for the devices being disposed of. Ensure serial numbers, condition notes, and planned disposal dates are populated before exporting.

Enterprise level software

For larger or more complex collections, automated audit tools such as Lansweeper or Microsoft Intune can scan your network and capture device make, model, serial number, and operating system automatically. These tools work well for networked laptops and desktops, but will not capture off-network or non-networked equipment, so a manual walkthrough is still needed to complete the picture.

How to build a device inventory checklist

A practical device inventory checklist covers three phases: planning, physical inventory, and data quality review.

In the planning phase, decide which tool you will use, assign responsibility to a specific person – usually the IT manager or systems administrator – and identify all locations where equipment is held. This includes offices, server rooms, branch locations, and storage areas. Equipment in long-term storage is often the most poorly documented and the most likely to contain sensitive data from previous users.

In the physical inventory phase, walk through each location and record every device to be recycled. For devices where the serial number is not immediately visible, check the following locations: laptops typically have a sticker on the base; desktops show the serial number on the rear or inside the case; servers display it on the front or rear bezel and in remote management consoles; tablets and phones show it in Settings under About; monitors usually have it on the back behind the stand.

In the data quality review phase, check for missing serial numbers, duplicate entries, and condition notes that are incomplete. Contact previous users for any missing details, particularly for devices that have been in storage for an extended period. Confirm the physical device count matches the number of rows in your device inventory checklist.

How to prepare equipment for disposal and collection

Alongside the inventory, there are practical steps to take before collection day to prepare equipment for disposal and ensure the process runs smoothly.

Power devices down at least 24 hours before collection. Group equipment by type – all laptops together, all desktops together – so that counting against your inventory is straightforward. Remove any personal items from device storage areas, including memory sticks in ports or items in printer paper trays. If any devices are missing batteries, power supplies, or cables, note this against the relevant inventory record so your recycler is aware in advance.

Label removable components such as loose hard drives or accessories to match the device they belong to, particularly for servers and desktop towers where internal drives may have been removed separately. If a device has a physical fault – a cracked screen, a failed power button, or a unit that will not start – attach a brief note describing the issue. This helps with sorting and segregation on arrival.

Bundle and label cables, power cords, and peripherals together by device where possible. Nothing should be loose or unattributed on collection day. Now you are good to go! Frequently asked questions

Do I need to wipe devices before collection?

No. We carry out all data sanitisation as part of the standard service using NCSC-approved methods. You do not need to take any action on devices before collection.

What if some of our devices are broken or very old?

Broken and legacy devices are accepted. Equipment does not need to be in working order to qualify for collection and recycling. If a device has ever held data, it will be processed as a data-bearing item regardless of condition.

Is storing old office IT assets an acceptable long-term solution?

No. Devices in storage still represent an active data liability and a continuing WEEE obligation. Disposal should not be deferred indefinitely. If you have a backlog of equipment that has been sitting in storage, we can arrange collection and deal with it in a single visit.

What documentation will we receive?

You will receive a Certificate of Recycling for all items collected and a Certificate of Data Destruction for each data-bearing device processed. These are the documents you need to retain in order to demonstrate compliance during an audit or investigation. Examples of our certificates are available on the website.

Does the correct process cost more than informal disposal?

Not necessarily. Our free collection service covers businesses with at least ten qualifying items. The certificates, data destruction, and WEEE-compliant processing are all included. The cost of getting it wrong – through ICO investigations, Environment Agency enforcement, or audit findings could be considerable.
