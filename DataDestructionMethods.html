How do we destroy your data?

Data sanitisation is the process of treating data on storage media so it can't be retrieved or reconstructed. It covers everything from a simple overwrite to complete physical destruction, and which one applies depends on the device, its condition and how sensitive the data on it is.

Our approach follows the principles set out in NCSC guidance on secure sanitisation and disposal of storage media, the benchmark for secure data sanitisation in the UK. This applies across both our standard and enhanced service levels, whichever method below is used for a given device.

Data Wiping

Our preferred method wherever reuse is possible. We use NCSC-approved erasure software to overwrite the entire accessible memory space, so the original data can never be recovered, but the device itself can be used again. This is the option that supports refurbishment and reuse rather than scrapping, and it's central to how we put circular economy principles into practice.

Degaussing

Used on magnetic media, such as hard disk drives and tapes, where software-based erasure isn't suitable. This destroys data by eliminating the magnetic field on the device. It doesn't work on solid-state drives or flash-based media, and it renders the device unusable, so it's a less sustainable option than wiping.

IT Asset Shredding

IT asset shredding. Physical destruction, breaking devices into fragments no larger than 6mm, the same benchmark set out in NCSC guidance. This is used when a drive can't be wiped or degaussed, and completely destroys the platters, mechanisms and electronic components. Where possible, data is still erased before shredding, as an added layer of assurance.

Every drive we collect, including USB drives, hard drives, SSDs and removable media such as CDs, tapes and floppy disks, goes through one of these processes. Where a device can be wiped rather than destroyed, we prioritise that route, since it's the better outcome for the circular economy as well as for cost.

What accreditations back our data destruction process?

Our data destruction processes are backed by:

  • ICO registration for data protection
  • Environment Agency Waste Carriers Registration
  • Waste exemptions covering secure storage and the repair or refurbishment of WEEE
  • NCSC-approved data erasure software and CPNI-approved shredding technology
  • Membership of TechUK and the Federation of Small Businesses
  • Full insurance, including employers' liability, public liability, products liability and professional liability

NCSC also runs a Sanitisation Assurance (CAS-S) scheme for providers working with government customers, a useful reference point if you're comparing sanitisation providers against a recognised standard.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between data wiping and degaussing?

Data wiping overwrites a drive with NCSC-approved erasure software so the device can be reused. Degaussing destroys the magnetic field on the drive so it can no longer be used at all. We only degauss where wiping isn't a suitable option.

Can data ever be recovered after wiping?

No. NCSC-approved erasure software overwrites the drive so completely that the original data can't be recovered, even though the physical device remains intact and usable.

Do you provide evidence of your accreditations?

Yes. Our Environment Agency Waste Carriers Registration and ICO registration numbers are published on our accreditations and compliance page, and available on request.

What happens if a drive can't be wiped or degaussed?

It goes for physical shredding, which breaks the device into fragments no larger than 6mm, destroying the platters, mechanisms and electronic components completely.

Are your methods compliant with government and public sector requirements?

Yes. Our processes are approved by the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), which is increasingly required for public sector and other regulated procurement.

Do you destroy data from damaged or non-functioning devices?

Yes. Even if a device won't power on, we can still destroy any data held on its storage media, usually via degaussing or shredding.
